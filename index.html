Who I Am

I am Nodjirambaye Penset Eric — an economist, educator, and aspiring AI researcher with roots in Chad and Nigeria. With over a decade of teaching experience, a BSc in Economics, an Associate Degree in Computer Science in progress at the University of the People (UoPeople), and certifications in French language education (FLE) and ESL from the British Council, I bring a unique interdisciplinary lens to the intersection of technology and development.

Recently, I completed the Foundations of Responsible AI certification at Northeastern University and the Data Science Bootcamp at AI Planet, deepening my expertise in ethical AI development and practical data science applications for social impact.

My current research focuses on how artificial intelligence shapes youth employment and digital divides in Nigeria — work that has been submitted to the OMEE journal and reflects my commitment to practitioner-informed, policy-relevant scholarship.

I am also an advanced French speaker (Open University UK) and believe deeply in the power of education as a tool for equity — particularly for African students navigating global scholarship and research opportunities.

FAQ

Common Questions

Answers to questions I receive most often about research, education, and collaboration.

Can you mentor me on my scholarship application?
Yes, I offer limited mentorship slots for African students applying to MS/PhD programs in AI, data science, economics, and education. Priority goes to applicants from underrepresented regions and those with demonstrated financial need. Contact me with your background and goals.
Are your Python and data science courses really free?
Yes, my foundational courses in Python, SQL, and data analysis are completely free. I believe education should be accessible, especially for African students facing connectivity and financial barriers. Premium mentorship and advanced courses may have fees in the future.
How can I collaborate on your research?
I'm open to research collaborations on AI and labor markets, digital divide, EdTech, and financial inclusion in Africa. Ideal partners have complementary skills (e.g., statistics, policy analysis, fieldwork experience) and share a commitment to practitioner-informed, ethical research.
What makes your scholarship guidance different?
Unlike generic advice, my guidance is rooted in deep research into African-specific challenges: information asymmetry, weak referee networks, financial documentation hurdles, and visa complexities. I also share what I wish I knew when I started my own journey.
Do you speak French? Can you help Francophone African students?
Yes, I'm an advanced French speaker (FLE-certified, Open University UK). I can provide guidance in French and have particular insight into the challenges faced by Francophone African students navigating Anglophone-dominated scholarship and research ecosystems.
